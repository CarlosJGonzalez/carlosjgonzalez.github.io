Sbeeqo Integration

Live Digital Waitlist on a Restaurant Website

Live: parguitog.com → · Platform: Seated →

The Client

Parguito Gourmet serves artisanal empanadas and Venezuelan-Caribbean cuisine in Hollywood, Florida, with a 4.4-star rating from more than 1,100 Google reviews. Its bilingual (EN/ES) website shows the menu, the dining room and online ordering.

The Challenge

Busy walk-in hours meant guests waited at the door with no clear idea of how long it would take, and staff had to manage the line by hand. The restaurant wanted guests to see the current wait and join the line before arriving, right from its own website.

The Solution

I connected the site to Seated, my multi-tenant waitlist platform, with a single embedded script. The widget sits in the hero section, next to "Order Online".

  • Live status: shows how many parties are waiting, or "Tables available now — no wait" when the dining room has room.
  • Inline join form: name, phone, optional email and party size, with a close button and a notice that the table is held for 5 minutes once called.
  • Phone call when ready: guests see the number they'll be called from, and Twilio calls them with the restaurant name, their name and the host's note.
  • QR at the door: walk-ins join the same list from their phones.

Square POS Integration

Parguito's Square account feeds the wait estimate. Today's open dine-in checks are compared with the restaurant's table count. Online and to-go orders are ignored. Square webhooks (order.created, order.updated) on the restaurant's own signed address update the estimate as soon as a check opens or closes. The credentials are encrypted with AES-GCM and stored only on the server.

Integration Details

  • One <script> loader plus a <div data-seated-waitlist="…"> placeholder. The widget is served from sbeeqo.com, so updates reach the site without code changes.
  • The React site injects the script after the page loads, because script tags written in JSX don't run.

Results & Learnings

  • Guests see a real wait (or "no wait") before they leave home, instead of guessing at the door.
  • The host runs the whole line from a tablet: Notify, Seat, Remove and a hold timer.
  • An embed-first design let a real restaurant adopt the platform without rebuilding its website.
